## ANALYSIS

A genuine ramp deck — Birds of Paradise, Werebear x2, Wild Growth x2, Nature's Lore, and Krosan Restorer accelerate toward Kamahl, Fist of Krosa (a repeatable land-animation anthem) and Stonewood Invoker (a literal 7-mana firebreathing sink), with Exploration adding extra land drops and Sylvan Library providing card advantage to smooth the climb. The manabase is 15 basic Forests with zero nonbasics, to maximize Gauntlet of Power's basic-land trigger.

**Gauntlet of Power is symmetric** — same caveat as the mono-red and mono-black siblings: neither the +1/+1 anthem nor the mana-doubling clause is restricted to "you control." It boosts any player's green creatures and basic Forests.

**Wild Growth + Gauntlet of Power stack, confirmed by both self-grill agents independently.** Wild Growth's "additional {G}" trigger and Gauntlet's "additional one mana" trigger are two separate triggered abilities off the same land tap, not a replacement effect. A Gauntlet-boosted, Wild-Growth-enchanted Forest taps for 3 green mana total (base + Wild Growth + Gauntlet).

**Self-grill revisions applied:** The Challenger found Mind Stone — a common ramp rock — sitting unused in the pool while the deck ran the strictly weaker, off-theme Emerald Charm. Swapped it in; Mind Stone is also resilient to the creature/enchantment removal that threatens 6 of the deck's 8 ramp pieces. In the sideboard, Seton's Desire's "race" framing didn't fit a deck whose default plan is attrition and big bodies, not aggression — swapped for Invigorating Boon, which turns the sideboard's own cyclers (Break Asunder, Lull, Elvish Aberration) into a minor +1/+1 counter engine for long grindy games. Elvish Spirit Guide's role was also mislabeled as "vs Control/Combo" when it's really a pure acceleration tool with no interactive text — corrected to "explosive ramp burst" framing rather than swapped out, since the card itself is a fine include.

**Land count honesty:** 15 lands (37.5%) is below the standard 38-42% Midrange band, justified by real ramp density (8 pieces: Birds of Paradise, Werebear x2, Wild Growth x2, Nature's Lore, Krosan Restorer, Exploration). The Challenger's caveat is fair: 6 of those 8 ramp pieces are creatures or auras exposed to removal, so losing an early ramp piece hurts more in a 15-land deck than a 16-land one. Fa'adiyah Seer's land-filtering partially offsets flood/screw risk, but this is a real fragility, not a free lunch.

**Mana-hungry top end, but not load-bearing:** Kamahl's ultimate ({2}{G}{G}{G} on top of his own 6-mana cost) and Stonewood Invoker's {7}{G} pump represent a genuine 9-11 mana aspiration — but both cards function as reasonable stand-alone bodies before those thresholds, and the deck has independent win paths (Giant Spider x2, kicked Kavu Primarch x2, Terravore, Symbiotic Beast's tokens, Call of the Herd) that don't require reaching them. Reaching 5-7 mana by turn 5-6 is realistic with 8 ramp sources; the full mana-sink payoffs are a late-game upside, not a prerequisite.

### Cards Considered but Excluded

- **Rares/mythics cut by the 5-card cap:** Worldly Tutor, Jolrael Mwonvuli Recluse, Arboria, Forgotten Ancient, Saproling Symbiosis, Nut Collector (mythic). Forgotten Ancient in particular was a close call — a mana-neutral scaling threat that would have fit the grindy plan well, but lost out to Kamahl/Sylvan Library/Exploration for the cap slots.
- **Uncommons/commons a tier below the cut:** Crop Rotation (land-sac cost fights the 15-land, no-nonbasics manabase), Slippery Karst and other nonbasic lands (excluded from the manabase entirely since they wouldn't trigger Gauntlet's basic-land clause), Wild Dogs (rejected — its text can hand control of the creature to an opponent who pulls ahead on life), Emerald Charm (cut for Mind Stone), Seton's Desire (cut for Invigorating Boon).
- **Sideboard-consideration cards not included:** Jolrael and Worldly Tutor (both rare, cap-blocked).

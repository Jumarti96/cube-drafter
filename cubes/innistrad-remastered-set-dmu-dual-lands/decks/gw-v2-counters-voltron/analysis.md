## ANALYSIS

### DECK IDENTITY

GW keyword-Voltron tempo. The deck concentrates permanent size on one or two bodies - Travel Preparations gives four castings of two +1/+1 counters, Lunarch Mantle is a permanent +2/+2 with a repeatable evasion outlet, Gryff's Boon adds flying and buys itself back out of the graveyard - and then Odric, Lunarch Marshal converts that single enhanced creature into a whole-team effect: 'creatures you control gain first strike until end of turn if a creature you control has first strike. The same is true for flying, deathtouch, double strike, haste, hexproof, indestructible, lifelink, menace, reach, skulk, trample, and vigilance.' One Twinblade Geist makes the team double strike; a Gryff's Boon, Niblis of the Urn or Restoration Angel makes it fly; Thalia makes it first-strike. ### READ THIS FIRST — TWO HONEST DISCLOSURES

**1. This deck carries the "+1/+1 Counters" theme the most thinly of the four.** Cards that actually generate a permanent counter: `Travel Preparations` ×2 (four castings), `Duel for Dominance` ×2 (coven half), `Hopeful Initiate` (training) — **5 of 24 nonland cards**. The Phase 5B critique traded some of the counters theme for keyword-sharing and blink because that is where the pool's power was, and that trade is a real departure from the archetype's name. It is disclosed here rather than buried.

**2. It shares 20 of its 24 nonland copies with the other two GW builds.** Genuinely unique to this deck: `Gryff's Boon` ×2, `Niblis of the Urn`, `Odric, Lunarch Marshal` — 4 copies, 16.7%. The differentiation is **interaction density** (9 of 24, 37.5%, against the Human midrange build's 7) plus a four-copy keyword-sharing package, at 15 creature copies against the aggro build's 18 and an average mana value of 2.167 against 2.375 and 2.391. It is a real axis and it is narrow. It is also the only one of the four where **coven is a designed subsystem rather than incidental**, and that was earned by a Phase 9 repair.

### THE ENGINE: ODRIC TURNS ONE KEYWORD INTO THE TEAM'S

Oracle: *"At the beginning of each combat, creatures you control gain first strike until end of turn if a creature you control has first strike. The same is true for flying, deathtouch, double strike, haste, hexproof, indestructible, lifelink, menace, reach, skulk, trample, and vigilance."*

| Keyword | Donors in this deck | Copies |
|---|---|---|
| Flying | Gryff's Boon ×2, Restoration Angel, Niblis of the Urn, Lunarch Mantle (for a turn) | 5 |
| Double strike | Twinblade Geist ×2 | 2 |
| First strike | Thalia, Heretic Cathar | 1 |
| Lifelink | Ambitious Farmhand's coven side (needs {1}{W}{W} + coven) | 2 |
| **Hexproof** | **none — zero donors** | **0** |

**10 copies across 4 keywords; 7 unconditional across 3.** One Twinblade Geist plus Odric makes every attacker double-strike. One Gryff's Boon plus Odric makes the whole board fly, which is how a stalled ground board becomes lethal in a single attack. Note the hexproof row: `Sigarda` was cut, so any claim that Odric shares hexproof here is false.

### THE COVEN FIX — THE MOST IMPORTANT REPAIR IN THIS BUILD

Both Phase 9 agents independently found that **coven was unsatisfiable**. Coven reads *"three or more creatures with **different** powers"*, and the creature base had only **two** distinct printed powers:

| Power | Copies (pre-repair) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 9 — Thraben Inspector ×2, Hopeful Initiate, Twinblade Geist ×2, Ambitious Farmhand ×2, Fiend Hunter ×2 |
| 3 | 5 — Cathar Commando ×2, Thalia, Odric, Restoration Angel |

Four of 24 nonland copies — half the archetype the user named — were gated behind seven other cards resolving first. `Guardian of Pilgrims` (a 2/2) was added purely to supply the third printed power, and `Lunarch Mantle`'s permanent +2/+2 supplies a fourth. Coven now switches on by deploying creatures, which it structurally could not do before.

A related correction worth knowing at the table: `Gryff's Boon`'s **+1/+0 is not filler**. A 1/1 becoming a 2/1 is the cheapest third-distinct-power maker in the deck.

### PLAY NOTES

- **Fiend Hunter + Restoration Angel is permanent exile — but the sequencing matters.** Blink Fiend Hunter in response to its **enters** trigger, so the leave-the-battlefield trigger resolves having exiled nothing. Responding to the *leave* trigger does not work; the card comes back.
- **`Angelic Purge`'s sacrifice cost:** pay with a Clue from Thraben Inspector or a spare Guardian of Pilgrims. Never the enhanced body.
- **Boarding in `Soul-Guide Gryff` ×2 and `Slayer of the Wicked` ×2** swaps four cards averaging ~2.1 mana for four averaging 4.5, pushing the deck's average past 2.5. Add a 17th land when bringing in all four.
- **`Overgrown Farmland` costs a rare slot** — the only deck of the four that spends one on a land. With four one-drops and fourteen two-drops, untapped-green by turn two was only 60.7% on two Radiant Groves; a land that comes in untapped from the third land onward was worth more here than a marginal fifth spell.

### WHAT THIS DECK GIVES UP

**Exile beats it.** An opposing `Fiend Hunter` or `Faith Unbroken` takes the carrier and every +1/+1 counter on it, and with `Sigarda` cut there is no hexproof anywhere in the 40. That is accepted, not solved.

**Only two of five enhancer copies survive the carrier's death** — `Gryff's Boon` ×2, which rebuy from the graveyard for {3}{W}. Travel Preparations' counters die with the body, and `Lunarch Mantle` is an Aura that goes to the graveyard with the creature it enchants. Cutting `Stitcher's Graft` (an Equipment, which would have persisted) improved the deck but made *this* specific failure mode worse, and that is stated in the failure-mode table rather than glossed.

## ANALYSIS

### DECK IDENTITY

GW Human tribal midrange that wins by out-sizing rather than out-numbering. Sixteen of the seventeen creature cards are Humans, and Metallic Mimic naming Human makes every one of them that arrives afterwards enter carrying a permanent +1/+1 counter; Mayor of Avabruck adds a static, retroactive +1/+1 on top. Hamlet Captain and Intrepid Provisioner turn that base into combat swings, Butcher's Cleaver reads 'as long as equipped creature is a Human, it has lifelink', and Thalia, Heretic Cathar taxes every opposing deployment while the deck deploys ahead of it. Where the aggro Torens build spends its cards on board count, this one spends them on card advantage - Thraben Inspector, Duskwatch Recruiter, Tireless Tracker and Ulvenwald Mysteries all convert lands, spare mana and dead bodies into cards - and on six creature-answering spells plus Faith Unbroken's exile, so it can trade one-for-one all game and still be the deck with the larger board on turn seven.

### READ THIS FIRST — WHAT THIS DECK IS AND IS NOT

This is the **Human tribal** deck of the four, not the Training-and-Coven deck. Both Phase 9 agents independently flagged the same thing and it is worth stating plainly rather than burying:

| Mechanic | Cards in the whole cube | Cards in this deck |
|---|---|---|
| Training | 2 (Hopeful Initiate, Torens) | **1** — Hopeful Initiate |
| Coven | 2 (Ambitious Farmhand, Duel for Dominance) | **2 of 2**, at 4 copies |
| Cards that place a permanent +1/+1 counter | — | **5 copies** (Metallic Mimic, Tireless Tracker, Hopeful Initiate, Duel for Dominance x2) |
| Cards that read "Human" | — | **6 copies of payoff**, against a 16-of-17 denominator |

The Phase 9 Challenger verified that this is the maximum obtainable: the pool holds exactly two training cards and two coven cards, the companion aggro build took Torens, and every non-rare GW card that places a permanent counter and is not already here is a non-Human that all four of this deck's payoffs miss simultaneously (Young Wolf, Festerhide Boar, Lumberknot, Moldgraf Millipede). The archetype is served as well as the pool and the five-rare cap permit. What actually holds the deck together is the Human web, and that web is dense.

### THE TWO PAYOFFS ARE COMPLEMENTARY, NOT REDUNDANT

This is the interaction the deck is built on, and the wording matters:

- **Metallic Mimic**: "Each other creature you control of the chosen type **enters with** an additional +1/+1 counter on it." A replacement effect on entry. It is **prospective** — it pays only for Humans deployed after it resolves, and never for itself.
- **Mayor of Avabruck**: "Other Human creatures you control get +1/+1." A static anthem. It is **retroactive** — it pays immediately for everything already on the battlefield.

Deploy Mimic on turn two and Mayor later, or the reverse, and the deck gets a different kind of value each time. Neither is a substitute for the other, which is why both occupy rare slots.

Two consequences worth knowing at the table:

1. **Mayor makes coven easier, not harder.** A uniform anthem shifts every other Human by the same +1, preserving the number of distinct powers, and Mayor itself stays a 1/1 because of "other" — so it *adds* a fourth distinct power to a 1/2/3 board. Only Mimic can compress the spread, and only when every creature arrived after it.
2. **Watch the Werewolf back faces.** Duskwatch Recruiter and Mayor of Avabruck both read "At the beginning of each upkeep, if no spells were cast last turn, transform this creature." A flipped Mayor **loses** the Human anthem and pumps Wolves and Werewolves, of which this deck has none. Holding up Clear Shot and Duel for Dominance on an empty opposing turn is how you flip your own lord off. This is a genuine tension between the interaction plan and two of the deck's own cards, and there is no fix for it beyond playing around it.

### THE CLUE LOOP

Four cards make Clues — Thraben Inspector x2, Tireless Tracker, Ulvenwald Mysteries — and two cards care when one is sacrificed: Tireless Tracker puts a +1/+1 counter on itself, Ulvenwald Mysteries makes a 1/1 white **Human** Soldier. `Lunarch Mantle`'s "{1}, Sacrifice a permanent: This creature gains flying until end of turn" is the third consumer. So a single activation can buy flying, a permanent counter and a Human body off one Clue — and that Human enters, so with Mimic out it arrives as a 2/2.

### WHAT THIS DECK GIVES UP

**Its two payoffs are a 2/1 and a 1/1.** Both die to anything. There is no noncreature +1/+1 counter payoff available within the rare budget — Cathars' Crusade would be a sixth rare and is in the companion aggro build. The deck's answer is not resilience but redundancy of a different kind: Duskwatch Recruiter x2 digs for a replacement at a 98.9% hit rate, and with seven interaction spells the deck can win a long game on cards rather than on the payoffs surviving.

**One mainboard answer to a noncreature permanent.** Hopeful Initiate, at {2}{W} plus two +1/+1 counters removed — and those counters are the deck's own win resource. Against artifacts and enchantments (49 of 277 cube cards, 17.7%) the real answer is boarding in Cathar Commando x2 and Angelic Purge x2.

**The sideboard is 9 of 10 white** in a deck whose pip demand is exactly even. That is a pool artifact rather than a choice: the GW pool's graveyard hate, tribal removal and artifact answers are all white. `Ambush Viper` is the one green card the pool offers for a sideboard and it is in there.

### HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM THE TORENS AGGRO BUILD

Honest accounting, since these two decks come from the same 109-card GW pool: roughly **19 of the 23 nonland cards would also be correct in the aggro build**. The separation rests on one real axis and four cards, not on four axes:

- **Interaction density — 7 of 23 nonlands here versus 3 in the aggro build.** This is the whole difference and it genuinely changes how games are won: trade-and-grind rather than curve-out-and-swing.
- **Four cards no aggro build wants:** Ulvenwald Mysteries, Tireless Tracker, Lunarch Mantle, Faith Unbroken. The last two are auras, and a 16-land go-wide deck does not spend cards on auras.
- Land count (17 vs 16) and average MV (2.391 vs 2.375) are **not** meaningful differentiators and are not claimed as such. A 0.016 difference in average mana value is noise.

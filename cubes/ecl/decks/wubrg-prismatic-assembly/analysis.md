## ANALYSIS

### DECK IDENTITY

The literal reading of the archetype: a genuine WUBRG manabase built so the best Vivid card in every colour can be cast. Where the two-colour build fakes five colours with hybrid cards, this one pays for them - five untapped rare duals, five common duals, an Eclipsed Realms, an Evolving Wilds and six basics - and is rewarded with the mono-coloured Vivid cards no other build can play: Shimmercreep drains X and gains X, Squawkroaster is an X-power double striker, Kithkeeper makes X two-coloured tokens, Explosive Prodigy is X damage on a body. The colour count itself is carried by cheap two-colour permanents - Figure of Fable is green and white on turn one, Mischievous Sneakling is blue and black on turn two - and capped by Tam, whose '{T}: Target creature you control becomes all colors' sets X to 5 from turn three onward.

### WHAT THIS DECK BUYS, AND WHAT IT PAYS

This is the literal reading of the Vivid archetype: build a manabase that produces all five colours, and you unlock the mono-coloured Vivid cards nobody else can play. `Shimmercreep` drains X and gains X. `Squawkroaster` is a double striker whose power *is* X - ten damage at X=5. `Kithkeeper` makes X tokens that are each green **and** white, so they raise X for everything cast afterwards. `Explosive Prodigy` is X damage to a creature, on a body, for two mana.

The price is exact and worth stating: **nine rare/mythic cards**, five of which are lands. Every untapped dual in this cube is a rare. Under your original 5-card cap this deck could not exist - the shocklands alone would have consumed the entire budget and left nothing for `Bloom Tender`, `Sanar` or `Tam`. This is the build the cap would have broken, which is why it is the clearest test of lifting it.

### THE MISTAKE THAT ALMOST SHIPPED

The first version of this list had a defect worth recording, because it is the trap this archetype sets. I had reasoned: *this deck makes five colours of mana, so the colour count takes care of itself.* That is a category error. Producing five colours of **mana** is not the same as controlling five colours of **permanent** - lands are colorless, and X counts permanents.

Measured against the first draft, the cheapest permanent supplying white, blue or black cost **four mana**. X therefore sat at 2-3 through the middle turns, and `Shimmercreep` drained for 2 instead of 5. The repair was not to re-price the cards but to fix the curve:

| Card | Cost | Colours it supplies | Turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Figure of Fable | {G/W} | green **and** white | 1 |
| Tam, Mindful First-Year | {1}{G/U} | green and blue, then **all five** | 2 |
| Mischievous Sneakling | {1}{U/B} | blue **and** black | 2 |

The measured effect: keepable opening hands rose from **78% to 84%**, and hands with a turn-one play from **0% to 20%**.

### TAM IS NOT REDUNDANT HERE

My own first sweep cut `Tam, Mindful First-Year` on the grounds that a real five-colour deck does not need a colour-fixer. Wrong, for the same reason as above. Tam's second ability reads "{T}: Target creature you control becomes all colors until end of turn" - it sets X to **5**, for free, every turn, and X is read by **11 of the 22 nonland cards** in this list: `Shimmercreep` x2, `Squawkroaster` x2, `Kithkeeper`, `Explosive Prodigy` x2, `Bloom Tender`, `Shinestriker`, `Prismatic Undercurrents`, `Sanar`.

### THE MANABASE IS THE DECK

Eighteen lands, eleven of them duals. Five enter untapped for two life; five enter tapped unconditionally; `Eclipsed Realms` enters untapped and, naming Elemental, produces any colour for **12 of the 22 nonland cards** - including both red cards, which is why red can safely run at four dedicated sources. Six basics are in the list on purpose: `Prismatic Undercurrents` reads "search your library for up to X **basic** land cards", and on an all-dual manabase that would be nearly blank. Shocklands are *Land - Forest Plains*; that makes them Forests and Plains, but not **basic**.

### HOW IT COMPARES TO THE TWO-COLOUR BUILD

| | Five-colour (this deck) | Two-colour Hybrid Prism |
|---|---|---|
| Rare/mythic cards | 9 | 3 |
| Keepable hands | 84% | 87% |
| Lands entering tapped | 5 of 18 unconditionally, 5 more at 2 life | 2 of 18 |
| Vivid cards it can play | all of them | only the G/U ones |

The five-colour build pays three percentage points of consistency, five rare slots and ten life across a game, and buys `Shimmercreep`, `Squawkroaster`, `Kithkeeper`, `Explosive Prodigy` and `Sanar` - five payoffs the two-colour deck cannot cast at all.

### WHAT IT CANNOT DO

It plays **no counterspell**. Every counterspell in the pool is blue, and holding up {U}{U} on an opponent's turn is incompatible with a manabase that must make every land drop on curve off tapped duals. The deck answers permanents after they resolve instead - `Crib Swap` exiles anything, `Nameless Inversion` kills three toughness for two mana, `Pyrrhic Strike` handles artifacts, enchantments or a 3-plus drop, and `Liminal Hold` exiles any nonland permanent at all.

### FAILURE MODES

| Mode | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| flood | mitigation | Prismatic Undercurrents reads 'You may play an additional land on each of your turns', so extra lands become development; Kithkeeper's 'Tap three untapped creatures you control: This creature gets +3/+0 and gains flying' and Figure of Fable's three-stage pump ladder ({G/W} to 2/3, {1}{G/W}{G/W} to 4/5, {3}{G/W}{G/W}{G/W} to 7/8 with protection from each opponent) are both open-ended mana sinks; and Sanar's main-phase trigger converts a flooded board into free spells each turn. |
| screw | accepted | 5 of 18 lands enter tapped unconditionally and 5 more are shocklands that cost 2 life to enter untapped. Mitigating would mean replacing duals with basics, which directly removes the colours that make Shimmercreep, Squawkroaster and Kithkeeper castable - the cards that are the entire reason to build this deck instead of the two-colour version. The bounded consolation is that the curve now starts at one (Figure of Fable), Bloom Tender and Great Forest Druid each add mana of any colour, Eclipsed Realms enters untapped, and the goldfish check measures 84% keepable hands and 92% three-lands-by-turn-three. |
| decapitation | mitigation | No single card holds the plan. The payoff suite is 7 reliability-weighted copies across 5 names (Shimmercreep x2, Squawkroaster x2, Kithkeeper, Sanar, Shinestriker) and the colour-supply suite is 7 more across 6 names. Tam is the highest-value single target, but losing it costs one path to X=5 out of a list that supplies green on 6 cards, red on 5, blue on 5, black on 4 and white on 3 independently of it. |
| gas-out | mitigation | This is the lens the build was chosen for. Shinestriker draws X on entry; Sanar reads 'At the beginning of your first main phase, reveal cards from the top of your library until you reveal X nonland cards... you may exile a card of that color... You may cast the exiled cards this turn' - a recurring free-cards engine scaling with the same variable as everything else; and Prismatic Undercurrents puts up to X basic lands into HAND. Resource ledger, stated with its limit: 3 of 22 nonland cards are Cards: Net-Positive (Sanar, Shinestriker, Prismatic Undercurrents), and one of those three refills MANA rather than threats, capped at the 6 basics in the list. |
| raced | accepted | The deck's first interaction is Nameless Inversion on turn two. Mitigating would mean maindecking one-mana answers, and the pool's cheap removal (Requiting Hex at {B}, Sear at {1}{R}) demands exactly the single coloured pips a manabase with 5 unconditional-tapped duals is worst at producing on turns one and two. The bounded consolation is that Shimmercreep gains X life on entry, Liminal Hold gains 2, Figure of Fable's 4/5 and 7/8 modes block anything in the cube, and the sideboard holds Luminollusk ('Deathtouch / you gain life equal to the number of colors among permanents you control'), Requiting Hex and Sear for the matchups where racing is the actual problem. |
| disruption-fizzle | mitigation | The value is front-loaded onto enters-the-battlefield triggers, so removal after the fact does not undo it: Shimmercreep's drain, Shinestriker's cards and Kithkeeper's tokens all persist. Stated with its limit - this covers 4 of the 7 payoff copies; Squawkroaster x2 sets its power with a STATIC ability that removal undoes entirely, and Sanar triggers only at the beginning of a main phase. Against a counterspell the deck simply has more: 7 weighted payoff copies, a 14-card-deep look by turn 7, and Sanar generating a fresh castable card every main phase it survives. |

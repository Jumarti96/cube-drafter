## ANALYSIS

### DECK IDENTITY

WB Blight-Shed Beatdown — the fastest of the four builds this cube supports in these colours. ECL prices a whole class of creatures two to three mana below rate and pays for it with -1/-1 counters that the creature then sheds on its own: Encumbered Reejerey is a 5/4 for {1}{W} that sheds a counter 'whenever this creature becomes tapped', so attacking is what grows it; Creakwood Safewright is a 5/5 for {1}{B} that sheds at end step while an Elf card is in your graveyard; Reluctant Dounguard is a 4/4 for {2}{W} shedding on every other creature entering; Burdened Stoneback is a 4/4 for {1}{W} whose counters convert into indestructible grants. Blight is a resource here rather than a tax, but only within limits the Phase 9 grill forced into the open: none of the eight shedders survives a blight 2 in its entry state, so the safe receivers are shedders that have already shed, Retched Wretch (for whom carrying a counter is the exact condition to return itself when it dies), and Moonlit Lamenter, the one body in these colours that survives a blight 2 (a printed 2/5, it enters at 1/4 and lands at -1/2, so it lives at toughness 2 while contributing nothing to the clock) and converts each counter it absorbs into a card. The deck curves out with stats two to three turns ahead of the opponent and kills on turn 5.

### KEY MECHANICAL OBSERVATIONS

**Blight is a resource here — but only within limits that had to be computed, not asserted.** The cube's blight mechanic reads "put N -1/-1 counters on a creature you control." The whole premise of this deck is that those counters land on creatures that remove them anyway. The Phase 9 Challenger tested that by computing entry-state toughness rather than counting cards, and found the premise half-false: Encumbered Reejerey enters as a **2/1** and dies to blight 1; Creakwood Safewright, Burdened Stoneback, Reluctant Dounguard and Heirloom Auntie all enter as **2/2** and die to blight 2. **Zero of the eight shedders can absorb a blight 2 in its entry state.** The safe receivers are a shedder that has already shed, Gutsplitter Gang itself, Retched Wretch (which wants to die holding a counter), and Moonlit Lamenter. That finding cut a Gutsplitter Gang and added two Moonlit Lamenters.

**The capacity arithmetic, stated properly.** Two Gutsplitter Gangs demand four counters every first main phase, recurring. Each shedder removes at most one counter per turn, and three of the five shed conditionally — Creakwood Safewright needs an Elf card in the graveyard, Reluctant Dounguard needs another creature to enter, Heirloom Auntie needs another creature to die. A realistic turn-5 board supplies about two counters per turn. Four demanded against two supplied is a standing deficit, and the alternative branch is three life per Gang per turn. One Gang is the number this board can actually pay for.

**Moonlit Lamenter is the card this deck didn't know it needed.** A 2/5 that enters as a 2/4: it survives Gutsplitter Gang's blight 2 at 2/2, and *"{1}{W}, Remove a counter from this creature: Draw a card"* turns the very counters the deck is forced to place into cards. It answers the blight-capacity problem and the gas-out problem with the same card, which is why two copies displaced a Gutsplitter Gang and a Moonglove Extractor.

**Changeling works in the graveyard.** Creakwood Safewright sheds *"if there is an Elf card in your graveyard"* — card, not creature, and not battlefield. Nameless Inversion is a Kindred Instant with changeling, and changeling is a characteristic-defining ability that applies in every zone, so a Nameless Inversion sitting in the yard **is** an Elf card. Together with Creakwood Safewright ×2, Moonglove Extractor, Dawnhand Dissident and Rhys (all printed Elves) that is 7 Elf-card copies of 23, and two cards actively dig for one: Dawnhand Dissident's surveil and Heirloom Auntie's per-death surveil.

**Encumbered Reejerey is the whole thesis in one card.** *"Whenever this creature becomes tapped while it has a -1/-1 counter on it, remove a -1/-1 counter from it."* The trigger is *becoming tapped*, not attacking, not connecting — so declaring it as an attacker fires it at declare-attackers, before blockers exist, whether or not it gets blocked. Left alone it goes 2/1 → 3/2 → 4/3 → 5/4 and deals 12 damage across turns 3–5 from a two-mana card with no support. It is also why the rejected evasion build was wrong: the tap has already happened, so giving it flying adds damage but zero shed events.

### COUNT-DEPENDENT VERDICTS

- BLIGHT CAPACITY, the finding the Phase 9 Challenger forced and the most important number in this deck. Blight 2 puts two -1/-1 counters on ONE creature you control, and a creature at 0 toughness dies. Computed entry-state toughness: Encumbered Reejerey enters 2/1 and dies to blight 1; Creakwood Safewright, Burdened Stoneback, Reluctant Dounguard and Heirloom Auntie all enter 2/2 and die to blight 2. So 0 of the 8 shedders can absorb a blight 2 in its entry state, and 2 of 8 die to blight 1. The safe receivers are: a shedder that has ALREADY shed (4/4 -> 2/2, 5/5 -> 3/3), Gutsplitter Gang itself (6/6 -> 4/4, but dead on its third trigger), Retched Wretch (4/2 -> 2/0 dies, which is the point, because it returns), and Moonlit Lamenter. Moonlit Lamenter is printed 2/5 and enters with one -1/-1 counter, so it is a 1/4 on entry — NOT the 2/4 both I and the Phase 9 Challenger first wrote, because a -1/-1 counter reduces power as well as toughness. ### FAILURE MODES

| Mode | Verdict | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| `flood` | accepted | this deck has almost no mana sink and that is the direct cost of the locked lens. Its curve tops at mana value 4 with exactly 1 copy there, and the repeatable outlets are Rhys's {W} activation, Dawnhand Dissident's {T} Blight 1, and Moonlit Lamenter x2's '{1}{W}, Remove a counter: Draw a card' — the last of which is the only one that converts mana into cards, and only while it carries counters. Mitigating flood properly would mean adding a sink at 4-plus mana value, which is exactly what the shape judge rejected in both rival sketches as converting an aggressor into a midrange deck that misses turn 5. What the deck buys instead is the lowest land count consistent with its curve, computed at 17. |
| `screw` | mitigation | 4 of 23 nonland cards cost 1 and 10 cost 2, so 13 are unconditionally castable on two lands and a 14th (Bogslither's Embrace) is castable only with a creature already on the battlefield to pay its blight. Only 2 of the 17 lands are tempo-negative (Sunlit Marsh x2) — the locked sketch cut Evolving Wilds outright for entering tapped and the shape judge credited that. Goldfish returns 87% keepable, an 88.1% three-lands-by-turn-3 rate, and a 58% turn-1 play rate. |
| `decapitation` | mitigation | there is no key card to answer. The thesis is carried by 8 shedder copies across 5 distinct cards, and no single removal spell changes the plan — killing one Encumbered Reejerey leaves 7 other shedders including a second Reejerey. This is the one deck of the four with genuine redundancy rather than a singleton engine, which is a direct consequence of spending only 3 of 5 rare slots. |
| `gas-out` | mitigation | 3 of 23 nonland copies draw cards after the Phase 9 repair. Moonlit Lamenter x2 is the specific fix, and it is doubly on-plan: it is the only body in these colours that survives Gutsplitter Gang's blight 2 — printed 2/5, it enters at 1/4 and a blight 2 leaves it at -1/2, alive at toughness 2 but with no power, so it is a draw engine rather than part of the clock, and every counter it absorbs becomes a card for {1}{W}. The pre-grill version of this entry ACCEPTED gas-out on the claim that 'the cheapest real card advantage available in these colours is Blighted Blackthorn at 5 mana value' — the Challenger showed that was false, and the acceptance has been replaced with a real mitigation rather than a rewritten excuse. Retched Wretch x2 additionally return themselves once they carry a counter, so 2 of the 23 cards are worth two bodies each, though the returned copy loses all abilities and so does it once only. |
| `raced` | mitigation | this deck is the fastest thing in these colours, so it usually IS the aggressor: a 58% turn-1 play rate, 96% by turn 2, and 12 of 23 nonland cards are creatures with printed power 4 or greater. | `disruption-fizzle` | mitigation | the deck has no critical turn to disrupt — it is not an assembly. Every one of its 8 shedders operates alone, and the sole two-card interaction (Rhys stripping a shedder's counters) is an accelerant rather than a requirement: without Rhys, Encumbered Reejerey still reaches 5/4 by attacking three times. Removal traded one-for-one against a two-mana 5/4 or 5/5 loses the exchange on mana. The real exposure, stated because the Challenger raised it and it belongs somewhere: the cube holds 48 cards that traffic in -1/-1 counters, and a Darkness Descends or Blight Rot aimed at a shedder in its 2/1 or 2/2 entry state kills it outright. Perfect Intimidation x2 ('Remove all counters from target creature') was added to the sideboard for exactly that, since the mainboard's only counter-stripper is a single sorcery-speed Rhys activation. |

### BUILD DERIVATION

- **Skeleton selection (Phase 5B Step 0):** chose *Sketch A — lowest-curve / most explosive* over *most reach & evasion* and *most resilient to removal and sweepers*. Judge grounds: The locked thesis is that the kill comes from bodies priced 2-3 mana below rate that shed their own counters through normal aggression — not a removal thesis, an evasion thesis, or a resilience thesis. A converts that literally: nine to ten MV-2 bodies means the turn-2 slot is almost never empty, and Reejerey's shed is attack-driven, so every point of the clock also un-shrinks the clock. One Reejerey alone is 3+4+5 = 12 damage across turns 3-5 with no second card, which is why goldfish 5 is plausible here and speculative in the rivals. The judge explicitly CREDITED A's Threats over-band on the grounds that in this pool the threat and the payoff are printed on the same card, and explicitly REJECTED B's interaction over-band on the same test.
- **Weak keystone — Nameless Inversion (as 'finisher pump'):** the text is +3/-3. Pointed at an un-shed body it kills your own creature — a turn-2 Encumbered Reejerey is a 2/1, so +3/-3 makes it 5/-2 and it dies. The pump role only exists on a creature that has already shed every counter. → ROLE REWRITTEN, card kept at 2 copies. Its role in the final list is removal plus an Elf card for Creakwood Safewright's graveyard condition — the two things its text actually supports. The pump line is real only on a fully shed creature and is not counted anywhere in this record.
- **Weak keystone — Creakwood Safewright (as 'turn-2 clock'):** its shed is gated on an Elf card in your graveyard, and the sketch's only stated Elf source was Nameless Inversion inside a 3-card interaction slot. Until one is in the yard it is a static 2/2 for two — below rate, not ahead of it. → DENSITY FIXED rather than the card cut. The final list carries 7 Elf-card copies: Moonglove Extractor x2 and Dawnhand Dissident are printed Elves, Nameless Inversion x2 is a changeling and therefore an Elf card in the graveyard, and Creakwood Safewright x2 are Elves themselves. Dawnhand Dissident's '{T}, Blight 1: Surveil 1' and Heirloom Auntie's per-death surveil both actively mill toward one. Safewright is still weighted 0.6 in the assembly check because the gate is real.
- **Weak keystone — Burdened Stoneback (as 'protection'):** indestructible is granted only at sorcery speed, so it cannot be held up against removal or a sweeper on the opponent's turn; it only pre-empts on your own main phase. It also does nothing against -1/-1 counter effects, which are this cube's main sweeper style. → ROLE REWRITTEN, card kept at 1 copy. It is recorded as a 4/4 body for two whose counters convert into indestructible grants on your own turn — which is exactly what an attacking deck wants before an alpha strike into open blockers. It is not claimed as protection against anything the opponent casts on their own turn.
- **Weak keystone — Rhys, the Evermore:** the persist half of the card is dead by construction — persist checks 'if it had no -1/-1 counters on it' and every relevant creature in this deck enters with two or three. Roughly half the card is blank. The advertised 'turn-three 5/4' also requires Rhys resolved by turn 2, since the ability taps and Rhys must be free of summoning sickness. → KEPT at 1 copy with both limits recorded. It is in the list purely for '{W}, {T}: Remove any number of counters from target creature you control', which is the only effect in the pool that can strip a shedder's whole counter load in one activation.
